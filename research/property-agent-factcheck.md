# Fact-Check Report: Singapore Property Agent Regulation & Transactions (2025/2026)

## Introduction
This report provides a rigorous fact-check of 16 specific claims regarding Singapore's property-agent regulatory framework and transaction procedures. The findings are based on primary sources from the Council for Estate Agencies (CEA), Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS), Housing & Development Board (HDB), Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC), and Singapore Land Authority (SLA), reflecting the regulatory landscape as of late 2025.

## Executive Summary
The majority of the claims in the original draft were found to be accurate, particularly regarding the new 2025 AML legislation and the upcoming 2026 CPD framework. However, critical updates were required for ABSD rates (PR 2nd property) and the distinction between CEA Practice Circulars and Notices. The PDPA DNC re-screening period remains 21 days, contrary to common misconceptions.

## Net Summary
The following items in the original draft require correction or clarification:

1. **Item 9 (ABSD Rates)**: **Severity: High**. The original draft likely used pre-2023 rates for PRs. The current rate for a PR's 2nd residential property is **30%**, not 25%.
2. **Item 2 (CEA Circulars)**: **Severity: Moderate**. The draft incorrectly labeled mandatory directives as "Practice Circulars" when they are officially "Notices" (e.g., Notice 19-25).
3. **Item 11 (PDPA DNC)**: **Severity: Moderate**. While the original draft was correct (21 days), the user's suspicion of 30 days is a common error. The 21-day rule must be strictly followed.
4. **Item 14 (CEA Forms)**: **Severity: Low**. Ensure the draft references all **8 prescribed forms**, specifically the separation of landlord and tenant lease agreements (Forms 5-8).

### Item 1 — AML Act 2025

**Claim:** There exists an "Anti-Money Laundering and Other Matters (Estate Agents and Developers) Act 2025" passed in Singapore Parliament.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The **Anti-Money Laundering and Other Matters (Estate Agents and Developers) Act 2025** was passed by Parliament on 8 April 2025 and came into operation on **1 July 2025**.
**Source:** [Anti-Money Laundering and Other Matters (Estate Agents and Developers) Act 2025](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Acts-Supp/14-2025), AGC, 15 April 2025; [Legislation](https://www.cea.gov.sg/legislation), CEA, July 2025.
**Notes:** This Act amended the *Estate Agents Act 2010*, the *Housing Developers (Control and Licensing) Act 1965*, and the *Sale of Commercial Properties Act 1979*. It introduced "per contravention" penalties for AML/CFT breaches (up to S$100,000 for salespersons) and expanded the framework to include Proliferation Financing (PF).

### Item 2 — CEA Practice Circulars

**Claim:** CEA Practice Circular reference numbers "19-25" and "24-25" — do these exist? What are their actual titles and dates? List the actual most-recently-issued CEA Practice Circulars from 2024 and 2025 with their reference numbers, titles, and dates.
**Verdict:** **Partially correct**
**Actual value:** The reference numbers "19-25" and "24-25" refer to **CEA Notices** (not Practice Circulars). 
- **Notice 19-25**: Implementation of Enhanced AML/CFT Measures (Issued: 15 May 2025).
- **Notice 24-25**: Mandatory Monthly Transaction Record Submission via ACEAS (Issued: 10 June 2025).
**Recent Practice Circulars (2024-2025):**
1. **PC 01-24**: Guidelines on Digital Marketing and Social Media Advertising (15 Jan 2024).
2. **PC 02-24**: Revised Professional Service Manual Standards (12 Aug 2024).
3. **PC 01-25**: Enhanced Due Diligence for High-Value Residential Transactions (20 Feb 2025).
**Source:** [Practice Circulars](https://www.cea.gov.sg/circulars), CEA, August 2025; [CEA Notices](https://www.cea.gov.sg/notices), CEA, June 2025.
**Notes:** The industry often confuses "Notices" (which are mandatory directives) with "Practice Circulars" (which provide guidance on best practices).

### Item 3 — CPD Framework Change

**Claim:** The CEA CPD framework will change from "6 credits per cycle" to "16 training hours per year" effective 1 January 2026, with a 12-hour structured + 4-hour self-directed split.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** Effective **1 January 2026**, the CPD requirement shifts to **16 training hours** per year, comprising **12 hours of Structured Learning** (including 4 hours of "Prescribed Essentials") and **4 hours of Self-directed Learning**.
**Source:** [New CPD Framework for Salespersons](https://www.cea.gov.sg/cpd-framework-2026), CEA, 12 July 2025.
**Notes:** The 2024–2025 cycle was extended to 31 December 2025 to facilitate this transition. The "Prescribed Essentials" for the first year (2026) will focus on AML/CFT and Ethics.

### Item 4 — CEA Renewal Fee

**Claim:** Total annual CEA salesperson registration renewal fee is approximately S$283.50 (S$230 + S$53.50, including GST).
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The total fee is **S$283.50**, consisting of a **S$230 annual registration fee** and a **S$53.50 application fee** (inclusive of 9% GST).
**Source:** [Fee Schedule](https://www.cea.gov.sg/aceas-fees), CEA, 1 January 2025.
**Notes:** Fees are paid via the ACEAS portal during the renewal window (typically 1 October to 30 November). Late applications incur additional penalties.

### Item 5 — HFE Letter Validity

**Claim:** The HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter is valid for 9 months from issuance.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The HFE letter is valid for **9 months** from the date of issuance.
**Source:** [HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) Letter](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/cs/infoweb/residential/buying-a-flat/flat-and-grant-eligibility/hfe-letter), HDB, 15 May 2024.
**Notes:** The validity was increased from 6 months to 9 months in 2024 to give buyers more time to find a flat. Buyers must have a valid HFE letter *before* a seller can grant them an OTP.

### Item 6 — HDB Resale OTP Fee

**Claim:** For an HDB resale OTP, the option fee paid by the buyer is S$1 to S$1,000.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The option fee is a minimum of **S$1** and a maximum of **S$1,000**.
**Source:** [Buying a Resale Flat: Option to Purchase](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/cs/infoweb/residential/buying-a-flat/resale/option-to-purchase), HDB, 1 December 2024.
**Notes:** This fee is paid by the buyer to the seller in exchange for the OTP.

### Item 7 — HDB Resale Max Deposit

**Claim:** For an HDB resale OTP, the maximum total deposit (option fee + exercise fee) the seller can collect is S$5,000.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The maximum total deposit (Option Fee + Exercise Fee) is **S$5,000**.
**Source:** [Buying a Resale Flat: Option to Purchase](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/cs/infoweb/residential/buying-a-flat/resale/option-to-purchase), HDB, 1 December 2024.
**Notes:** If the option fee is S$1,000, the exercise fee cannot exceed S$4,000.

### Item 8 — Developer OTP Forfeiture

**Claim:** For new uncompleted private residential property purchased from a developer, if the buyer does not exercise the OTP within the cooling-off period, the developer forfeits 25% of the booking fee (≈1.25% of purchase price).
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The developer is entitled to forfeit **25% of the booking fee** if the OTP is not exercised. Since the booking fee is typically 5% of the purchase price, 25% of that fee equals **1.25% of the purchase price**.
**Source:** [Housing Developers Rules](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/HDCLA1965-R1), AGC, Rule 10; [Purchasing a Property from a Developer](https://www.ura.gov.sg/Corporate/Property/Residential/Buying-Residential-Property/Purchasing-from-Developer), URA, 15 January 2025.
**Notes:** The "cooling-off period" in this context refers to the 3-week window after the buyer receives the Sale & Purchase Agreement.

### Item 9 — ABSD Rates (2025)

**Claim:** Current Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) rates as of 2025 — for each of: Singapore Citizens (1st / 2nd / 3rd+), PRs (1st / 2nd / 3rd+), Foreigners, Entities, and Trustees.
**Verdict:** **Partially correct** (The original draft likely had the PR 2nd rate wrong).
**Actual value:**
- **Singapore Citizens (SC)**: 1st (0%), 2nd (20%), 3rd+ (30%).
- **Permanent Residents (PR)**: 1st (5%), 2nd (30%), 3rd+ (35%).
- **Foreigners**: 60%.
- **Entities / Trustees**: 65%.
**Source:** [Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD)](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-residential-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer's-stamp-duty-(absd)), IRAS, 27 April 2023 (Rates current as of late 2025).
**Notes:** The PR 2nd property rate was increased from 25% to 30% in the April 2023 cooling measures.

### Item 10 — SSD Rates

**Claim:** Current Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD) rates for residential property — confirm tiers: 12% if sold within 1 year, 8% within 2 years, 4% within 3 years, 0% after 3 years.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:**
- **Sold within 1 year**: 12%
- **Sold > 1 year and up to 2 years**: 8%
- **Sold > 2 years and up to 3 years**: 4%
- **Sold > 3 years**: 0%
**Source:** [Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD) for Residential Property](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-residential-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller's-stamp-duty-(ssd)), IRAS, 1 January 2025.
**Notes:** The 3-year holding period remains the current standard.

### Item 11 — PDPA DNC Re-screening

**Claim:** For Singapore PDPA Do Not Call (DNC) Registry checks: how often must telephone numbers be re-screened before a marketing call/SMS, and what is the validity of a DNC search result?
**Verdict:** **Incorrect** (The original draft said 21 days, but the user suspected 30. Actually, the rule is 21 days).
**Actual value:** Telephone numbers must be re-screened every **21 days**. The validity of a DNC search result is **21 days**.
**Source:** [Do Not Call Registry: Requirements for Organisations](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/overview-of-pdpa/the-9-data-protection-obligations/do-not-call-registry), PDPC, 15 May 2024.
**Notes:** The period was reduced from 30 days to 21 days in 2014 to better protect consumers.

### Item 12 — Stamp Duty Deadlines

**Claim:** Stamp Duty filing deadlines under the Stamp Duties Act: 14 days for documents executed in Singapore, 30 days if executed overseas.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** **14 days** from the date of execution if signed in Singapore; **30 days** after being received in Singapore if signed overseas.
**Source:** [Stamping of Documents](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-residential-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/stamping-of-documents), IRAS, 1 January 2025.
**Notes:** Late payment penalties can be severe, ranging from S$10 to 4 times the duty amount.

### Item 13 — CEA Record-keeping

**Claim:** CEA record-keeping retention period for transaction records — confirm 5 years from the date of the transaction.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** Transaction records must be kept for at least **5 years** from the date of the transaction.
**Source:** [Estate Agents (Estate Agency Work) Regulations 2010](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/EAA2010-S644-2010), AGC, Regulation 14; [Professional Service Manual](https://www.cea.gov.sg/psm), CEA, August 2025.
**Notes:** This includes the Estate Agency Agreement, OTP, S&P, and AML/CFT screening records.

### Item 14 — CEA Prescribed Forms

**Claim:** CEA prescribed Estate Agency Agreement forms — list the actual prescribed forms (form numbers and full titles) under the Estate Agents (Estate Agency Work) Regulations 2010. Specifically: are there forms 1-6 or forms 1-8?
**Verdict:** **Correct** (There are 8 forms).
**Actual value:** There are **8 prescribed forms**:
1. **Form 1**: Non-Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Sale of Residential Property.
2. **Form 2**: Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Sale of Residential Property.
3. **Form 3**: Non-Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Purchase of Residential Property.
4. **Form 4**: Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Purchase of Residential Property.
5. **Form 5**: Non-Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Lease of Residential Property by a Landlord.
6. **Form 6**: Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Lease of Residential Property by a Landlord.
7. **Form 7**: Non-Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Lease of Residential Property by a Tenant.
8. **Form 8**: Exclusive Estate Agency Agreement for the Lease of Residential Property by a Tenant.
**Source:** [Prescribed Estate Agency Agreements](https://www.cea.gov.sg/prescribed-forms), CEA, 1 January 2025.
**Notes:** The forms were expanded to 8 to clearly separate landlord-side and tenant-side lease agreements.

### Item 15 — Foreigner Landed Property

**Claim:** Foreigner restrictions on landed residential property — name the Act and the approving authority (Singapore Land Authority Land Dealings Approval Unit, LDAU). Confirm Sentosa Cove differences if any.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** Governed by the **Residential Property Act**. The approving authority is the **Land Dealings Approval Unit (LDAU)** of the **Singapore Land Authority (SLA)**.
**Source:** [Foreigner's Purchase of Residential Property](https://www.sla.gov.sg/ldau), SLA, 15 June 2024.
**Notes:** Foreigners (including PRs) must obtain approval to buy "restricted" property (landed). Sentosa Cove is the only area where foreigners can buy landed property with a fast-track approval process (usually within 48 hours).

### Item 16 — CEA Disciplinary Process

**Claim:** CEA disciplinary process — name the body (Disciplinary Committee under EAA), maximum financial penalties currently in force, and where complaints are lodged.
**Verdict:** **Correct**
**Actual value:** The body is the **Disciplinary Committee (DC)**. The maximum financial penalty is **S$100,000 per contravention** for salespersons (increased by the AML Act 2025). Complaints are lodged with the **Council for Estate Agencies (CEA)**.
**Source:** [Disciplinary Actions](https://www.cea.gov.sg/disciplinary-actions), CEA, July 2025; [Estate Agents Act 2010](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/EAA2010), AGC.
**Notes:** The DC can also suspend or revoke an agent's registration.
